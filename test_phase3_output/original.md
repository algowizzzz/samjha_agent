- Collateral should usually be acceptable based on internal rules.
- Valuations are performed, but the specific methodology and frequency of these valuations must be clearly defined.
- The policy must include detailed haircut schedules, outlining the specific haircut percentages applied to different asset types, as well as any exceptions to the standard haircut requirements.
- The policy must clearly define the specific requirements and criteria for when legal review is required for collateral acceptance, including any exceptions or escalation processes.
- The policy must include clear, measurable criteria for all collateral acceptance requirements, with defined roles, responsibilities, and escalation processes to ensure proper governance and traceability. ## 4. Roles & Responsibilities

## Roles are partially defined

The policy should clearly define the roles and responsibilities for all stakeholders involved in the collateral acceptance process, including but not limited to:
- Lending Desk: Responsible for onboarding new collateral, performing initial valuations, and monitoring collateral performance
- Credit and Risk: Responsible for setting collateral acceptance criteria, approving exceptions, and providing ongoing oversight and monitoring
- Legal: Responsible for reviewing collateral agreements and ensuring compliance with relevant regulations
- Senior Management: Responsible for approving the policy, monitoring compliance, and escalating issues as necessary ## 5. Operational Procedures

## Some steps are mentioned but lack structure

The policy should outline the detailed operational procedures for the collateral acceptance process, including but not limited to:
- Collateral onboarding and verification workflows
- Valuation methodologies and frequency
- Exception review and approval process
- Integration with enterprise-wide risk management and compliance controls ## 6. Exceptions & Escalations The policy should clearly define the exceptions and escalations process, including but not limited to:
- Criteria for exceptions (e.g., collateral types, valuation thresholds, etc.)
- Approval authorities and escalation path for exceptions
- Documentation requirements for exception requests and approvals ## 7. Compliance, Monitoring & Reporting The policy should outline the compliance monitoring and reporting requirements, including but not limited to:
- Key performance indicators and metrics to monitor policy compliance
- Reporting cadence and distribution to relevant stakeholders
- Evidence standards and documentation requirements
- Escalation process for non-compliance or policy breaches ## 8. Definitions & Interpretation A few terms are included but glossary alignment is missing. ## 9. Appendix Appendix materials need to be added.
